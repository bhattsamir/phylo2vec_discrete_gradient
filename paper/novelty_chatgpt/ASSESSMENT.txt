NOVELTY ASSESSMENT
Reviewed 3 November 2026. This is a focused comparison with primary literature
and the local implementation, not an exhaustive proof of priority.

RECOMMENDED CENTRAL CLAIM

We develop an exact BME coordinate evaluator and a parallel block-optimization
formulation. A shared quadratic computation evaluates all valid coordinate
changes. Applying it to an exactly condensed skeleton preserves the full-tree
objective differences while reducing the size of the coordinate problem.

The useful distinction is the combination of the evaluation algorithm, its
parallel dependencies, and its exact block restriction. Exact BME itself,
coordinate differences, relabeling, and fixed-clade reduction have precedents.
The current evidence supports an algorithmic and systems contribution. It does
not establish a new fundamental BME identity or a universal first claim.

CLOSEST PRIOR WORK

1. Phylo2Vec, Penn et al. (advance access 2024, journal volume 2025).
   Section 1.3.2, Equation 4 and Algorithm 1 already define the neighbour
   matrix, coordinate hill climbing, and ordered relabeling. The present
   distinction is the shared BME-specific evaluator, not these definitions.
   Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/html/2304.12693v3
   Published version: https://doi.org/10.1093/sysbio/syae030

2. GradME, Penn et al. (2023).
   The Methods and supplementary continuous-objective derivation give an
   expected BME objective and a cubic recurrence. Its exactness is not the
   distinction. Here evaluation is specialized to a discrete tree and includes
   the full coordinate range. Appendix A.4 now explains the vertex relation.
   Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05739v2
   Published version: https://doi.org/10.1093/gbe/evad213

3. Hordijk and Gascuel (2005), and FastME 2.0 (2015).
   Balanced subtree averages and exact SPR score propagation already permit
   quadratic sweeps. Our coordinate mapping and parallel organization do not
   improve that asymptotic order. They provide a different computational
   interface and execution schedule, with a different candidate subset.
   Primary text: https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/bti713
   Primary text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4576710/

4. Haws, Hodge and Yoshida (2011).
   Section 2.4 describes fixed-clade faces as affine images of smaller BME
   polytopes. The condensation identity should be presented as the explicit
   objective restriction used by our algorithm, not an unprecedented theorem.
   Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/html/1004.2073v2#S2.SS4

5. Tapo and Zhu (2026 preprint), scikit-bio BME.
   Parallel array-based BME already exists. The preprint abstract, author
   repository and official documentation identify taxon-addition construction
   as its task. Our task evaluates rearrangements of an existing tree.
   Read the primary abstract and implementation documentation. The full
   preprint could not be retrieved, so its internal claims were not audited.
   https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.27.740982v1
   https://github.com/qiyunzhu/skbio-bme-bench/
   https://scikit.bio/docs/latest/generated/skbio.tree.bme.html

6. Camerota Verdu et al. (2026).
   Their method learns an SPR selection policy, with online search and
   adaptation. This distinguishes search policy from the evaluation mechanism
   developed here. Their use of a GPU also rules out casual claims to be the
   first GPU-assisted BME search.
   Primary full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12814993/

WHAT THE MATHEMATICAL ARGUMENT ESTABLISHES

For a fixed component partition, the exact condensed matrix is sufficient
to score every reconnection of those components up to one constant. Therefore
the same is true of every skeleton coordinate change. This is deterministic
equality for the supplied distance matrix. It is stronger than an unbiased
estimate obtained by sampling leaf pairs. It does not require additive input
distances, and it does not imply global optimization.

The full coordinate table contains (N-1)^2 entries. An algorithm explicitly
writing these values takes at least quadratic work in the standard unit-cost
model. The shared evaluator meets that bound. This elementary observation
does not lower-bound best-move-only evaluation, and is not a new lower bound
for tree search in general.

BLOCKS AND COSTS

The exact dense-distance CPU minibatch path exists. The main theorem also
specifies a proper subtree window using the entire oriented outside component.
After a commit, the next window must use the updated tree.

O(K^2) is the cost of the reduced coordinate sweep AFTER exact distances are
available. It is not an N-independent cost for arbitrary raw distances.
The recorded large sequence runs use capped proposal distances followed by
full acceptance checks. They should not be advertised as timing the all-exact
proposal method. The appendix keeps that distinction explicit.

POSITIONING TO RETAIN

Lead with the computational operation made practical: many exact global
objective changes from one shared computation, or from a smaller exact block.
Then identify the concrete parallel dependencies and memory/recomputation
tradeoff. Treat the classical identities as the reason the method is correct.
Do not claim first exact BME, first vector search, first parallel BME, a new
condensation principle, a better-than-FastME asymptotic sweep, or guaranteed
coverage of tree space by the implemented greedy schedule.

SEARCH SCOPE AND LIMIT

Primary-source checks covered the original representation, continuous BME,
classical SPR evaluation, fixed-clade geometry, recent parallel BME, and
learned BME search. Searches for BME minibatches, discrete Phylo2Vec gradients,
and GPU BME did not reveal the precise combined formulation described here.
Absence from this search is not a proof that no earlier implementation exists.
